# 《归途：奥德修斯》V2 Production Bible

Status: `PASS_PRODUCTION_BIBLE`

## Format and production promise

- 30 episodes, five scenes per episode, 6:50–7:15 estimated finished runtime.
- 2.00:1 acquisition and delivery assumption; protect 16:9 center extraction for platform variants.
- M1 world: the mythic Mediterranean and active gods are real. Divine pressure changes weather, attention, coincidence, sound, or physical limits; it never performs the decisive human choice.
- Master cost grammar: medium. Scale is created by controlled foreground, partial construction, sound, light, editorial ellipsis, and repeatable blocking—not default full-CG creatures or fleets.
- Dialogue is recorded for close performance. Spectacle may widen the frame, but identity decisions return to faces, hands, objects, thresholds, and shared space.

## Tone and visual language

The series begins with a home occupied in plain sight, opens outward into stories that are beautiful and morally expensive, then compresses back into the same house. The visual question is always: who may occupy this space under what name?

- **Ithaca:** dry limestone, olive smoke, worn bronze, coarse wool, doorways that show who owns passage. Early frames place Telemachus at edges; later he occupies the center without taking his father’s marks.
- **Phaeacia:** pale fabric, polished wood, broad lateral movement, hospitable symmetry that becomes forensic scrutiny.
- **Wanderings:** stronger silhouettes and fewer complete horizons. Odysseus’ account is not fantasy gloss; its beauty makes his omissions more dangerous.
- **Return:** longer lenses, narrower corridors, foreground occlusion, repeated thresholds, exact prop custody.
- **Recognition:** camera distance tightens only when proof becomes private. Public claims are covered in groups; scar, bed, and orchard knowledge are covered through touch and object detail.

The camera does not announce a god with a generic orbit. It notices the condition change first—a stopped crowd, altered eye-light, a wind reversal, water pulling the wrong direction—then finds the divine body if one is present.

## Camera distance and movement

- Default dramatic coverage: medium two-shots and three-quarter singles that preserve hands, exits, and prop geography.
- Close-up is earned by irreversible evidence: the household seal, a rope-burned hand, Argos’ collar, the scar, bow fibers, bed-root joinery, bark marks, a spear released into soil.
- Handheld is limited to bodies losing negotiated control: raft break, cave blinding, fleet strike, Scylla grab, hall counterattack. It stops when a character regains a plan.
- Battle coverage maintains a stable north: dais, main door, side door, four columns, weapon stair, tables, and arrow line. No axis jump may obscure who controls an exit.
- Wide shots establish a production rule or consequence, not scenery. A wide may show an empty oar station, the narrow harbor trap, twelve axe heads, or two kin groups divided by a boundary stone.

## Editing rhythm

- EP01–04: 3–6 second average shot length under public pressure; longer takes only when an inexperienced Telemachus must continue speaking after losing support.
- EP05–15: action inserts and withheld reaction create unreliable testimony. Phaeacian listener reactions interrupt heroic continuity.
- EP16–25: fewer geographic resets; cuts follow keys, cups, weapons, wounds, and doors.
- EP26–28: action is cut by ammunition and access, not by arbitrary speed. Each depleted resource earns a new phase.
- EP29–30: hold after proof, but let civic danger interrupt emotional release. The last image remains labor—the new staff—rather than triumph.

## Production design and standing sets

Five standing-set families carry most of the season:

1. **S1 Ithaca palace compound:** hall, dais, four columns, service side, inner courtyard, weapon wall/store, stair, bedroom threshold, weaving gallery, and small mill insert. The set must support clean, occupied, disarmed, blood-stage, sulfur-cleaned, and restored states.
2. **S2 Phaeacian ceremonial hall:** banquet hall, queen’s work area, guest threshold, and courtyard. Redress supplies Pylos and Sparta interiors, Aeolus’ terrace, and selected Circe rooms without sharing signature props or palette.
3. **S3 Hero ship deck:** removable rails, mast, high rig section, nine marked oar positions, breakaway deck panel, wind-bag tie point, and black-water tank edge.
4. **S4 Eumaeus farm:** hut, pig gate, feed area, olive shelter, low wall, and approach path; built for day/night and rain control.
5. **S5 Adaptable shore/cave environment:** water edge, reversible cave mouth, two rock faces, reed/forest edge, and controllable horizon. Redress handles Ogygia, Scheria, Lotus coast, Cyclops shore, Aeaea, Thrinacia, Ithaca harbor cave, and parts of Underworld.

Additional location units are limited to assembly/athletic court, forest/road, black-stage Underworld trench, orchard/field, and controlled water insert tank. See `LOCATION_MATRIX.md`.

## Costume logic and color progression

- **Odysseus:** Trojan bronze and command red survive only as fragments. Ogygia drains him to salt grey; Phaeacian white is borrowed identity; beggar browns hide repaired blue thread; after recognition he does not return to pristine royal costume.
- **Penelope:** undyed household wool with a single purple year-thread. Public scenes add formal layers that conceal her working belt and keys. In EP29 she remains in household dress, not a reunion gown.
- **Telemachus:** oversized inherited belt and corrected fastenings early; travel adds practical knots and dust; return uses a self-repaired strap; final civic staff is new wood, not inherited regalia.
- **Athena:** one repeated pin/eye-light geometry crosses disguises. Costume matches the host community until a deliberate continuity error allows the viewer to recognize her.
- **Suitors:** individually legible appetites and wealth; colors crowd Odysseus’ emptied seat. Their finery becomes damaged, wet, and blood-dark during the hall battle.
- **Wanderings:** each island is differentiated primarily through fiber, wear, and silhouette rather than a wholly new costume world.

Color progression: Ithaca ochre/ash → Phaeacian chalk/blue → wanderings rust/black/poison green → return mud/olive → battle bronze/red → aftermath undyed white joined to old purple.

## Lighting

- Practical fire and Mediterranean daylight are motivated sources; eye-light is never a beauty exception without story cause.
- Athena’s presence adds a narrow cool return in eyes and polished metal, not a glowing body.
- Poseidon removes horizon separation and makes reflected water climb surfaces.
- Underworld has no visible sky direction; blood is the only warm reflective surface.
- Hall occupation is bright enough to see theft. Battle darkness comes from closed doors, smoke, and broken lamps, not underexposure.
- Marriage recognition uses the same oil-lamp direction as earlier interrogation; proof changes, not romantic lighting.

## Sea representation

The sea is a pressure system, not a sequence of expensive establishing shots. Use one hero deck, partial raft, low tank edge, wind, spray, wet costume continuity, horizon plates, miniature/scale fragments, and surround sound. Water contact is saved for irreversible losses. Deck movement, rig tension, and actors’ changing balance sell conditions before any exterior composite.

## Gods, sound, and music

- **Athena motif:** one dry spear-butt pulse plus a short inhaled female cluster; often begins in ordinary room tone.
- **Poseidon motif:** sub-bass oar stroke, rope strain, and water inside architecture.
- **Zeus:** pressure drop and one physically answered crack; no conversational cloud spectacle.
- **Recognition motif:** an object’s natural sound returns before music—the cup ring, bow string, basin rim, bed wood, bark scratch, staff saw.
- **Home motif:** an uneven five-beat loom rhythm becomes oar rhythm, then Telemachus’ staff-cutting rhythm.
- Score avoids continuous heroic uplift. Music yields to breath, tools, animals, and room response at choice points.

## Violence grammar

Violence is short, spatial, and consequential. Hits alter posture, inventory, exits, or trust. Blood is not decorative punctuation. The hall action follows exact arrow and weapon custody; Telemachus’ arm wound changes later throwing; Laertes throws once from prepared footing. Sexual threat and enslavement are represented through blocked doors, confiscated work, coercive gaze, and aftermath rather than exploitive coverage.

## Creature grammar

Mythic beings are presented through one undeniable physical fact plus withheld scale:

- Cyclops: human-scale performance enlarged by forced perspective, giant hand/foot/eye pieces, and low-frequency breath.
- Circe transformations: performer choreography, prosthetic mouth/ear fragments, shadows, bowls, and animal sound; restoration is equally physical.
- Sirens: voice and private memory in water reflection; bodies remain ambiguous.
- Scylla: six separate grabs, rigged high points, shadows, and one partial limb/maw; never a full creature beauty shot.
- Charybdis: deck inclination, debris circulation, water pull, and a composited vortex edge.

The full LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH options and locked medium choice are in `VFX_CREATURE_STRATEGY.md`.

## Recognition-object grammar

Recognition is an evidence ladder, not a string of sentimental props:

1. **Name:** spoken, withheld, falsely assigned, and catastrophically exposed.
2. **Story/clothes:** public testimony can be exact and still counterfeit identity.
3. **Argos/collar:** nonverbal bodily recognition; true but unavailable to a court.
4. **Scar:** bodily history; sufficient for intimates and loyal agents, insufficient for Penelope and Laertes.
5. **Bow/axes:** public competence and household custody; proves claim under witnesses, not marriage.
6. **Bed:** private construction knowledge tied to a living root; Penelope’s final human verification.
7. **Father/land:** orchard knowledge exceeds the scar and reconnects lineage to work.
8. **Community:** witnesses, assembly, boundary stone, and disarmament turn identity into accountable civic membership.

Each hero object has one custodian, a defined route, and a reset rule in `PROP_AND_HERO_ASSET_LIST.md`.

## Extras and casting strategy

- Four principals carry the season: Odysseus, Penelope, Telemachus, Athena.
- Sixty-five nonprincipal credited identities normalize to 28 featured supporting roles and 37 day-player, voice, creature, or group identities.
- One 28-person core ensemble supplies Ithaca assembly, suitors, Phaeacians, crew, dead, and kin through separated costume/hair blocks. No performer crosses identities within the same episode block.
- Crowd plates expand only where source scale matters. Foreground action is choreographed for 8–16 bodies; background extension never replaces named responsibility.

## Production constraints

1. No tracked V1 path is altered; production interpretation belongs to V2 only.
2. No full-CG creature or open-sea principal performance is required by the master draft.
3. Child, animal, water, fire, height, and weapon work receive separate units or controlled inserts.
4. EP26–28 require a locked floor plan, arrow ledger, weapon ledger, blood continuity board, and rehearsal before principal photography.
5. Shoot EP29 clean hall/bedroom material before EP26–28 damage and blood stages.
6. The bed root, bow, scar, cup, purple thread, keys, six bone counters, ship plate, and three spears are continuity-critical hero assets.
7. Divine VFX may intensify conditions but cannot obscure the human action that satisfies source fidelity.
8. Runtime cannot be filled with empty establishing footage; every held image carries evidence, task, threat, or consequence.
