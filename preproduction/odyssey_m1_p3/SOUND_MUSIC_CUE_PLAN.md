## Episode cue architecture

- **EP01–04:** household materials and public voice. Score avoids announcing Telemachus’ maturity before behavior earns it.
- **EP05–08:** sea, cloth and borrowed hospitality. Odysseus’ name disclosure removes masking reverb and makes the room acoustically accountable.
- **EP09–16:** voyage memory uses recurring ship inventory sounds; each loss subtracts a layer. Creature reveals are breath/contact-led.
- **EP17–24:** farm and hall details become forensic: gate, dog, portion knife, stool, keys, basin, scar and bow box.
- **EP25–28:** bow/axes/door/weapon inventory provides the battle clock. Music withdraws from first blood until the human trap closes.
- **EP29:** performance room—wood, cup, clothing and breath. No recognition chord before Penelope verifies the bed.
- **EP30:** dead voices dry, orchard numbers concrete, field weapons heavy; civic closure ends in ordinary work and shared room tone.

## Special recording

- Sirens: record each memory line dry, close, distant, water-reflected and reversed-tail. Crew POV contains no intelligible Siren language.
- Gods: record direct voice and environment response separately so the mix can retain agency in the human frame.
- Bow/axes/three spears: hero asset wild tracks at multiple distances and contact states; library substitutes are reference only.
- Hall battle: phase-specific wild tracks after each furniture/weapon reset, plus clean S1 room tones for S1-A through S1-F.
- Underworld: breath and cloth remain human; depth comes from placement and selective absence, not a continuous supernatural drone.

## Music constraints

No character receives a fixed heroic melody that guarantees moral approval. Themes change through rhythm, instrumentation and missing notes. Source events of recognition, death and forgiveness land first in performance/object sound; score enters after the irreversible action when needed.
