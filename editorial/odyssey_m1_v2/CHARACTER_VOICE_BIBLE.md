# 《归途：奥德修斯》Character Voice Bible V2

Status: `LOCKED_FOR_SCREENPLAY_V2`

The purpose of this document is audible separation. If names are covered, the listener should still recognize social position, habitual defense, and relationship history. No character owns “smart modern Chinese” as a shared house style.

## 奥德修斯 Odysseus

- **Speech rhythm:** scans first, answers second; under pressure he reduces to verbs and coordinates.
- **Sentence length:** adaptable—ornate when inventing biography, clipped when command is real.
- **Directness:** indirect until he has mapped power; brutally direct after exits are controlled.
- **Metaphor domain:** knots, routes, doors, weight, wind, tools; he turns people into navigable terrain when afraid.
- **Humor:** dry misclassification that makes an opponent reveal more.
- **Anger pattern:** becomes quiet and logistical; public boasting is the dangerous exception.
- **Never says:** “trust me” without supplying a usable detail.
- **Avoids naming:** guilt for men lost under his command; calls it weather, orders, or hunger until forced.
- **How he lies:** over-specific provenance, one true tactile detail inside a false route.
- **How he asks:** offers two answers and watches which one the other rejects.
- **How he threatens:** describes the opponent’s next available exit disappearing.
- **How he shows affection:** repairs, teaches, shares a plan, remembers a private object; direct tenderness arrives late and awkwardly.

## 佩涅洛佩 Penelope

- **Speech rhythm:** lets others overstate; returns with one concrete discrepancy.
- **Sentence length:** short questions in public, carefully nested conditions in private.
- **Directness:** direct about household action, indirect about hope.
- **Metaphor domain:** weaving, stores, measures, keys, debts, guest portions, the difference between pattern and thread.
- **Humor:** cool domestic correction that exposes a man’s ignorance of the house he claims.
- **Anger pattern:** assigns a task, changes access, or makes someone wait; volume rises only when her authority is publicly erased.
- **Never says:** “I knew it was you all along.”
- **Avoids naming:** her desire for Odysseus’ return; names risks and evidence instead.
- **How she lies:** constructs a rule that is technically true while keeping her intention private.
- **How she asks:** starts with an object—clasp, garment, bed, gift—then asks who could know it.
- **How she threatens:** withdraws recognition, hospitality, or the legitimacy a claimant came to obtain.
- **How she shows affection:** preserves a habit, remembers workmanship, gives another person room to prove rather than comforting them early.

## 忒勒马科斯 Telemachus

- **Speech rhythm:** EP01–04 starts too soon, corrects himself, and looks to the wrong person; later learns to pause and issue one usable instruction.
- **Sentence length:** early fragments and rehearsed speeches; midseason practical questions; late concise tactical calls.
- **Directness:** initially blurts the moral truth without a plan; grows into selective disclosure.
- **Metaphor domain:** doorways, borrowed chairs, missing place settings, reins, maps; he understands adulthood through things he has not yet been allowed to control.
- **Humor:** self-conscious, occasionally literal; never the effortless wit of his father.
- **Anger pattern:** voice cracks before it steadies; early anger overreaches, late anger protects secrecy.
- **Never says:** a polished thesis about manhood.
- **Avoids naming:** fear that Odysseus’ return could make him irrelevant.
- **How he lies:** too little detail early; later repeats a prepared cover exactly.
- **How he asks:** first asks permission, then testimony, then operational consequence.
- **How he threatens:** early, invokes his father; late, names what he himself will do.
- **How he shows affection:** tries to take on another person’s burden, sometimes clumsily; learns not to decide Penelope’s belief for her.

## 雅典娜 Athena

- **Speech rhythm:** arrives mid-action; gives one altered condition, never a full emotional debrief.
- **Sentence length:** compact imperatives, then a testing question.
- **Directness:** direct about tasks, oblique about divine motives.
- **Metaphor domain:** game boards, sight lines, disguises, leverage, weather seen from above.
- **Humor:** amused impatience at human self-deception.
- **Anger pattern:** removes help or reveals scale; does not shout.
- **Never says:** “everything will be fine.”
- **Avoids naming:** the limits Zeus places on her and her partial responsibility for delay.
- **How she lies:** changes appearance rather than facts; lets a mortal infer the wrong identity.
- **How she asks:** asks what the human will do if she changes one condition.
- **How she threatens:** makes absence visible—“try it without me.”
- **How she shows affection:** grants a tool, a moment of clarity, or a second attempt; expects the recipient to act.

## 安提诺俄斯 Antinous

- **Speech rhythm:** interrupts before a claim is complete; repeats the final noun as if he owns its definition.
- **Sentence length:** short public verdicts.
- **Directness:** openly coercive because the room has rewarded him.
- **Metaphor domain:** meat portions, seats, gates, ownership, appetite.
- **Humor:** makes the vulnerable person the room’s entertainment.
- **Anger pattern:** uses an object already in hand—cup, stool, food—before reaching for a weapon.
- **Never says:** “perhaps” or offers restitution.
- **Avoids naming:** fear that Telemachus is becoming legitimate.
- **How he lies:** converts occupation into hospitality and coercion into custom.
- **How he asks:** rhetorical demand with punishment already prepared.
- **How he threatens:** specifies what will be eaten, taken, or blocked tomorrow.
- **How he shows affection:** political favoritism through portions and seating; no vulnerable tenderness.

## 欧律马科斯 Eurymachus

- **Speech rhythm:** listens to the room, then supplies the version most likely to survive.
- **Sentence length:** smooth medium clauses with an escape hatch.
- **Directness:** indirect until Antinous is dead; then bargaining becomes explicit.
- **Metaphor domain:** exchange, price, public agreement, blame allocation.
- **Humor:** flattering imitation.
- **Anger pattern:** smile stays while hands recruit others; sudden violence when rhetoric fails.
- **Never says:** “I was wrong”; says “we were misled.”
- **Avoids naming:** his own initiative in the occupation.
- **How he lies:** tells each listener the most useful portion of a shared plan.
- **How he asks:** frames compliance as the other person’s clever idea.
- **How he threatens:** predicts isolation rather than promising direct harm.
- **How he shows affection:** gifts calibrated to debt; intimacy is transaction.

## 安菲诺摩斯 Amphinomus

- **Speech rhythm:** starts with group language, ends in first person when conscience catches up.
- **Sentence length:** hesitant, interrupted by qualification.
- **Directness:** capable of warning but not leaving.
- **Metaphor domain:** omens, thresholds, tomorrow, the last safe moment.
- **Humor:** uneasy deflection, rarely at another’s pain.
- **Anger pattern:** turns on the nearest louder suitor, then retreats.
- **Never says:** “I had no choice.”
- **Avoids naming:** that comfort matters more to him than principle.
- **How he lies:** postpones a decision and calls postponement prudence.
- **How he asks:** seeks one more sign.
- **How he threatens:** almost never; when cornered, invokes the group.
- **How he shows affection:** warns privately, too late to change public alignment.

## 欧迈俄斯 Eumaeus

- **Speech rhythm:** works while speaking; sentences end when the task does.
- **Sentence length:** plain medium phrases, occasional rural proverb earned by labor.
- **Directness:** direct about animals, food, weather, and injustice; guarded about hope.
- **Metaphor domain:** gates, herd behavior, cuts of meat, rain, bedding, dogs.
- **Humor:** practical undercutting—if a grand claim cannot feed or shelter someone, he says so.
- **Anger pattern:** protects first, curses second.
- **Never says:** “loyalty” about himself.
- **Avoids naming:** how abandoned he feels by Odysseus.
- **How he lies:** poorly and only to protect a person; his hands reveal discomfort.
- **How he asks:** offers food before questions, then asks origin while dividing portions.
- **How he threatens:** closes a gate, releases dogs, or states what livestock can do to a trespasser.
- **How he shows affection:** gives the better portion, the dry place, the only cloak.

## 欧律克勒娅 Eurycleia

- **Speech rhythm:** interrupts with remembered childhood names; age gives her permission to ignore rank.
- **Sentence length:** clusters of sensory memory, then a command.
- **Directness:** direct in service matters, tactically silent in danger.
- **Metaphor domain:** scars, baths, linen, infancy, household routines.
- **Humor:** proprietary embarrassment—she remembers heroes before they controlled their bladders.
- **Anger pattern:** names the exact neglected task or betrayal.
- **Never says:** “I serve whoever rules.”
- **Avoids naming:** fear she will outlive the house she raised.
- **How she lies:** resumes a routine so perfectly that observation looks foolish.
- **How she asks:** touches or arranges first, questions after recognition begins.
- **How she threatens:** promises household memory will survive the liar.
- **How she shows affection:** washes, covers, scolds, uses the childhood form of a name.

## 瑙西卡 Nausicaa

- **Speech rhythm:** thinks aloud once, then issues clean social instructions.
- **Sentence length:** quick practical clauses.
- **Directness:** direct about distance and reputation, indirect about attraction.
- **Metaphor domain:** laundry, games, roads, gossip, who walks beside whom.
- **Humor:** exposes adult ceremony as logistical fuss.
- **Anger pattern:** makes servants repeat an unkind act correctly.
- **Never says:** she has fallen in love with the stranger.
- **Avoids naming:** marriage anxiety.
- **How she lies:** changes the visible order of travel so gossip cannot assemble the truth.
- **How she asks:** gives a boundary and waits to see whether it is honored.
- **How she threatens:** withdraws escort or public cover.
- **How she shows affection:** supplies exact next steps without demanding gratitude.

## 阿尔喀诺俄斯 Alcinous

- **Speech rhythm:** ceremonial opening, generous offer, then a host’s boundary.
- **Sentence length:** balanced public clauses meant for witnesses.
- **Directness:** direct about hospitality, slower about suspicion.
- **Metaphor domain:** table, voyage, exchange of gifts, the city’s reputation.
- **Humor:** expansive host humor that lowers a room’s temperature.
- **Anger pattern:** stops music or games; does not compete for volume.
- **Never says:** “my wife is overcautious.”
- **Avoids naming:** fear Poseidon will punish Phaeacian transport.
- **How he lies:** calls a political risk a family welcome.
- **How he asks:** gives food first, then asks the question in front of all.
- **How he threatens:** removes the social protection of guest status.
- **How he shows affection:** seats, feeds, equips, and publicly vouches.

## 喀耳刻 Circe

- **Speech rhythm:** slow enough to make urgency look childish.
- **Sentence length:** sensuous lists followed by a hard monosyllabic condition.
- **Directness:** direct about appetite, evasive about captivity.
- **Metaphor domain:** cups, appetite, skin, seasons, doors between species and worlds.
- **Humor:** clinical amusement at men who call desire an external spell.
- **Anger pattern:** transforms the social body; rarely argues.
- **Never says:** “I made you do it.”
- **Avoids naming:** loneliness and the pleasure of being negotiated with.
- **How she lies:** gives an indulgence the grammar of treatment.
- **How she asks:** places an object within reach and watches whether it is taken.
- **How she threatens:** names the form a person’s appetite already resembles.
- **How she shows affection:** restores bodies, gives precise survival knowledge, releases without pretending release is painless.

## 卡吕普索 Calypso

- **Speech rhythm:** intimate repetition; she says a name to make departure feel like betrayal.
- **Sentence length:** long when arguing cosmic unfairness, very short when hurt.
- **Directness:** direct about desire, indirect about imprisonment.
- **Metaphor domain:** tides that return, weaving, immortality, rooms without clocks.
- **Humor:** bitter comparison between male and female gods.
- **Anger pattern:** stops helping with a shared task, then resumes because she cannot bear its failure.
- **Never says:** “you are free” before Zeus’ order.
- **Avoids naming:** that safety without consent is captivity.
- **How she lies:** makes permanence sound like protection from grief.
- **How she asks:** offers the thing no mortal should refuse and watches refusal wound her.
- **How she threatens:** describes what age and sea will do after he leaves.
- **How she shows affection:** teaches raft craft, packs food, and refuses to sentimentalize the coming pain.

## 波吕斐摩斯 Polyphemus

- **Speech rhythm:** names what he touches; repeats ownership; pauses only to eat.
- **Sentence length:** blunt fragments, expanding into prayer when wounded.
- **Directness:** absolute.
- **Metaphor domain:** hunger, herd, rock, smell, father.
- **Humor:** cruel literalism around guest-gifts.
- **Anger pattern:** strikes the environment until escape routes vanish.
- **Never says:** abstract moral categories.
- **Avoids naming:** dependence on Poseidon until helpless.
- **How he lies:** rarely; assumes strength makes truth irrelevant.
- **How he asks:** demands location, count, and ownership.
- **How he threatens:** announces order of consumption.
- **How he shows affection:** touches rams with surprising care; tenderness belongs to herd, not humans.

## 拉厄耳忒斯 Laertes

- **Speech rhythm:** slow inventory; memory returns through cultivation, not rhetoric.
- **Sentence length:** sparse until a plant name unlocks a sequence.
- **Directness:** direct about decay and work, cautious about identity.
- **Metaphor domain:** roots, grafts, rows, seasons, soil, scars on bark and body.
- **Humor:** dry correction of anyone who does not know how things grow.
- **Anger pattern:** old body becomes exact; one committed action rather than prolonged fury.
- **Never says:** “I always knew you would return.”
- **Avoids naming:** shame that grief withdrew him from the household crisis.
- **How he lies:** tests by pretending a memory is wrong.
- **How he asks:** asks for a tree’s location or history before a person’s claim.
- **How he threatens:** sets feet, chooses one spear, says the distance.
- **How he shows affection:** gives land knowledge, touches scar, corrects a remembered count.

## 波塞冬 Poseidon

- **Speech rhythm:** mostly nonverbal recurrence; when language occurs, it arrives in broken phrases carried by surf.
- **Sentence length:** elemental fragments, never conversational repartee.
- **Directness:** condition rather than argument.
- **Metaphor domain:** depth, pressure, salt, coastlines, debt carried by waves.
- **Humor:** none.
- **Anger pattern:** repeats a force at larger scale; does not chase through rooms.
- **Never says:** a motivational or thematic aphorism.
- **Avoids naming:** human domestic concerns except as the destination he can delay.
- **How he lies:** he does not; magnitude makes explanation unnecessary.
- **How he asks:** through impossible weather or a sudden absence of wind.
- **How he threatens:** makes the safe route behave like deep water.
- **How he shows affection:** toward Polyphemus, through answering the son’s precise curse.

## Voice collision rules

1. Odysseus may use a polished line only when performance is a tactic; the line must cost him information or trust.
2. Penelope wins through verification procedure, not superior aphorisms.
3. Telemachus’ syntactic control must visibly increase across EP01–04, EP18–20, and EP25–28.
4. Athena alters conditions; Eumaeus performs care; Eurycleia invokes memory. None may be interchangeable “mentor” voices.
5. Antinous owns; Eurymachus bargains; Amphinomus postpones. Suitor dialogue must preserve that three-way distinction.
6. Divine voices differ by medium: Athena is strategic interruption, Poseidon environmental pressure, Calypso intimate stasis, Circe appetite and transformation.
